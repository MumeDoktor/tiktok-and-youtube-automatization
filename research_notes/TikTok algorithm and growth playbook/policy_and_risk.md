## 1. AI-generated content (AIGC): what must be labelled, AI voice-over on archive footage, penalties, distribution, viewer controls and "AI slop"

### Takeaway
The written rule is narrow: a label is required only when AI or heavy editing is used "to show realistic-looking people or scenes", and the August 2026 Community Guidelines explicitly say disclosure is NOT needed for "generic text-to-speech (TTS) narration, when the TTS isn't a recognizable voice of a known individual" - so a generic TTS voice over genuine archive film does not require the AI label, but a cloned/recognisable voice does. TikTok says labelling does not reduce distribution, yet since November 2025 viewers can dial AI content down in Manage Topics, and 2026 press reports describe (without a primary TikTok source I could verify) further action against low-quality AI accounts.

### Cited Findings

**WRITTEN POLICY - Community Guidelines, "Edited Media and AI-Generated Content (AIGC)" (released 25 Aug 2026, effective 24 Sep 2026)**
- Core rule: "we require creators to label AI-generated or significantly edited content that shows realistic-looking scenes or people. Unlabeled content may be removed, restricted, or labeled by our team, depending on the harm it could cause." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- Definition: "AI-Generated Content (AIGC): Any image, video, or audio made or changed by AI. This can include realistic scenes or artistic styles, like anime, cartoons, or paintings." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- "Significantly Edited Content: Media that makes it seem like someone did or said something they didn't, or alters their appearance so much that they're unrecognizable. This includes: Cropping or cutting phrases to change meaning; Rearranging or combining clips; Changing speed or adding/removing audio or video parts". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- Required disclosure ("using the AIGC label or a clear caption, watermark, or sticker"): "You must label content that uses AI or includes significant edits to show realistic-looking people or scenes. You can add your own clear caption, sticker, or watermark. For AI-generated content, you can also use our AIGC label." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- "Disclosure is needed when content isn't harmful but could be confusing, including when: A face is replaced with someone else's; AI tools make it look like someone said something they didn't; A background, object, or person is added or removed in a misleading way; AI-generated audio mimics the voice of a real person". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- "Disclosure isn't needed when: Making small edits like color correction, reframing, or cropping; Using artistic styles, like anime; Using generic text-to-speech (TTS) narration, when the TTS isn't a recognizable voice of a known individual". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity). The same three bullets are repeated verbatim in the Creator Academy article "AI-generated content label" (refreshed 2026-09-26) — [Creator Academy: AI-generated content label](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/ai-generated-content-label)
- Not allowed even if labelled (extract): "Using the likeness of private figures without consent"; "AIGC or significantly edited content that misleads about a matter of public importance, such as: Content made to look like it comes from a real news source; A crisis event, like a natural disaster or conflict; A public figure being degraded, harassed, or linked to criminal behavior; A public figure taking political stances, supporting products, or commenting on public issues they haven't actually addressed". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- FYF-ineligible tier: "Any realistic-appearing content which isn't yet confirmed to be AIGC or significantly edited content, but presents matters of public importance in a way that could lead to misinterpretation, or cause harm to private figures". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- Related misinformation rule relevant to archive footage: FYF-ineligible content includes "Sharing unedited media that is presented out of context and may mislead a person about a topic of public importance"; and not allowed: "falsely presenting past crisis events as recent". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)

### Inferences
- Channel 1 (real US-government/Prelinger film + generic TTS + motion-graphics captions): under the text in force, no AI label is required, because the visuals are real footage and generic TTS is expressly exempt. A label WOULD be required if the voice is a clone or recognisable imitation of a real person (e.g. a period newsreel announcer or a named figure), or if any shot is AI-generated/AI-upscaled into something that reads as real footage (e.g. generated "archive" b-roll of a detonation).
- Applying the creator AI label to wholly real footage "to be safe" is not risk-free: TikTok calls mislabelling unaltered content a ToS violation, the label cannot be removed after posting, and labelled videos fall under the viewer-side "see less AI" slider. The safer voluntary disclosure for TTS narration is a caption/bio line rather than the platform AIGC toggle.
- Channel 2 (podcast clips) has an AIGC-adjacent exposure that is easy to miss: "Cropping or cutting phrases to change meaning" and "Rearranging or combining clips" are defined as significant editing. Tight cuts that change what a named guest appears to say move the clip into the labelled/FYF-ineligible/not-allowed tiers.
- The archive channel should date and source footage on screen: mis-contextualised unedited media is an FYF-ineligibility ground, and nuclear-test footage presented without a date could be read as a current event.
- C2PA auto-labelling is metadata-driven: if any tool in the pipeline (a generative image/video tool, some TTS or editing tools) attaches Content Credentials flagging AI generation, TikTok may auto-apply an irremovable "AI-generated" label regardless of the creator's choice.

### Gaps
- No TikTok primary source found that defines or names "AI slop" as a policy category; the August 2026 Community Guidelines do not use the term. Whether a synthetic-media classifier down-weights AI-flagged videos in ranking (claimed by a vendor blog in search results) is unverified.
- I could not open TikTok's own November 2025 newsroom post (URL guess failed), so the 1.3 billion figure and watermark details rest on secondary press.
- No published data on how often unlabeled-AIGC enforcement results in removal vs. a TikTok-applied label vs. an account strike.
- Whether TikTok's invisible watermark or classifiers detect third-party TTS audio is not documented.

## 2. Originality and unoriginal content: Community Guidelines and For You feed eligibility standards; what "meaningful transformation" means

### Takeaway
The written standard is that reused material is ineligible for the For You feed "without anything new" / "without creative edits", with someone else's watermark or logo named as the textbook example; TikTok never uses the phrase "meaningful transformation" and gives no quantitative threshold. The stricter test sits in the monetisation rules (Section 8), where speed changes, filters, fixed text and stickers are explicitly "only slight modifications", and an account that posts a lot of FYF-ineligible content can be made ineligible as a whole.

### Cited Findings

**WRITTEN POLICY - Community Guidelines, "Unoriginal Content and Intellectual Property Rights" (effective 24 Sep 2026)**
- Summary rule: "Unoriginal Content and Intellectual Property Rights: We don't allow content that violates intellectual property rights, including reposts of copyrighted or trademarked material without permission." — [Community Guidelines: Overview](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/overview)
- "You should only post content you created or have the right to share. We don't allow content that violates someone else's intellectual property (IP) rights. If we become aware of content that breaks these rules, we will remove it." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- "Content is also ineligible for the FYF if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- FYF INELIGIBLE list, verbatim and complete: "Reused or unoriginal content posted without creative edits, such as clips that show someone else's watermark or logo" and "Low-quality or minimally edited content, such as short clips made from GIFs only". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- The August 2026 text contains no reference to "QR codes" and no heading "reproduced content"; the watermark/logo example above is the only such wording in the current FYF standards (a search of the full extracted guideline text for "QR" returned nothing). — [Community Guidelines: For You feed Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)

**WRITTEN POLICY - For You feed Eligibility Standards (effective 24 Sep 2026)**
- "We make ineligible for the FYF certain content that may not be suitable for a broad audience. Learn more about the types of content we leave out from the FYF in the 'FYF Ineligible' sections throughout our Community Guidelines." — [Community Guidelines: For You feed Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- "Even if a video doesn't make it to the FYF, people may still find it through search or by going to a creator's account. If a video isn't getting many views, it also doesn't necessarily mean it broke a rule. Creators can check TikTok's analytics to see how their videos are performing, including if there were any made ineligible for recommendation." — [Community Guidelines: For You feed Eligibility Standards](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/fyf-standards)
- Account-level consequence: "Sometimes, accounts that don't break the rules still post a lot of content that's ineligible for the FYF. In those cases, we may make the account and its content ineligible for the FYF and harder to find." — [Community Guidelines: Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features)
- Monetisation link: "Content that isn't eligible for the FYF may also be restricted from monetization." — [Community Guidelines: Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features)
- Search: "some content that's allowed on TikTok may not be suitable for all audiences, and might not appear as a top result." — [Community Guidelines: Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features)

**WRITTEN POLICY - Creator Academy "Understanding TikTok's Originality Policy" (refreshed 2026-09-26)**

### Inferences
- TikTok's operative concepts are "anything new", "creative edits", "additional information and/or content value" and "original commentary, original perspective, original editing". In practice the archive channel's defensible "new" elements are an original script/narration, original research and structure, and original motion graphics; the podcast-clip channel's re-captioning and re-framing are the kind of change TikTok elsewhere calls "only slight modifications" (see Section 8), so on the written text it is the higher-risk account for FYF ineligibility.
- Source watermarks are the single most explicit trigger. Archive transfers often carry burned-in logos or slates (Getty/stock bugs, archive idents, AP/Periscope Film watermarks); podcast video usually carries the show's logo/lower-thirds. Both match "clips that show someone else's watermark or logo".
- "Content combined from multiple sources with little or no additional information" describes compilation edits; a montage of archive shots with only music would fit, a narrated explainer would not.
- Because repeated FYF-ineligible posts can make the whole account ineligible, a twice-daily automated cadence compounds risk: 60 posts a month of borderline-original content accumulates the pattern quickly.
- The web-only Content check lite is a usable pre-flight gate, but it is not reachable from the phone-automation posting path.

### Gaps
- No TikTok definition of "meaningful transformation", no minimum percentage of original material, and no statement on whether voice-over alone suffices.
- No enforcement statistics specific to "unoriginal content" FYF ineligibility were found in the sources read.
- Older commentary refers to FYF rules on "QR codes" and "reproduced content"; I could not confirm when that wording was removed (the archive of previous versions at https://www.tiktok.com/safety/en/policies-and-engagement/cg-archive was not read).

## 3. Copyright and clipping: IP policy, strikes, repeat-infringer bans, podcast clips, licensing norms, public-domain archive footage and music

### Takeaway
TikTok removes content on rightsholder report, issues one strike per removal, bans at three copyright strikes (strikes expire after 90 days), and reserves immediate bans for severe cases; it acknowledges fair use but says appeals based on "only a small portion" or "others post it too" are generally rejected. Unlicensed podcast clipping therefore depends entirely on the rightsholder not reporting, whereas US-government film is free of US copyright - but Prelinger material is not uniformly public domain and may contain third-party music.

### Cited Findings

**WRITTEN POLICY - Intellectual Property Policy (updated 27 Mar 2025, effective 26 Apr 2025)**
- "not all unauthorized uses of copyrighted content constitute an infringement. In many countries, exceptions to copyright infringement allow the use of copyrighted works under certain circumstances without authorization from the copyright holder. These include the fair use doctrine in the United States, permitted acts of fair dealing in the European Union (EU), and other equivalent exceptions under applicable local laws in other countries." — [TikTok IP Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en)
- "Any user content that infringes another person's copyright may be removed... We have adopted and reasonably implemented an intellectual property repeat infringer policy under which we, in appropriate circumstances, ban the account of a user who repeatedly commits copyright infringement. We may exercise our discretion to immediately ban any account in cases of severe copyright violations. We reserve the right to refuse any account holder whose account was used for improper activities from opening a new TikTok account." — [TikTok IP Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en)
- Appeals: "you can reach out to the copyright owner directly to request a retraction. You may also submit an appeal within the TikTok app, which can be accessed from the in-app notification sent to you... the United States DMCA or similar laws in other countries provide instructions on how an affected user can appeal a removal by submitting a compliant counter-notification... where appropriate and authorised by law, we will forward your entire appeal to the original reporter, including any contact information you provide". — [TikTok IP Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en)

**WRITTEN POLICY - Help Center "Copyright" (undated)**
- Strike mechanics: "Under our repeat infringer policy, we issue a strike to someone if their content was removed due to copyright infringement. Multiple reports leading to content removal within a short period of time may be counted as a single strike. There is a strike limit for each IP type, after which we'll permanently remove the account. We count strikes for copyright and trademark infringements separately. For example, if you obtain 2 strikes for copyright infringement and 2 strikes for trademark infringement, your account won't be banned. However, if you obtain 3 strikes for copyright infringement and one strike for trademark infringement, your account will be banned. Accrued strikes will expire from your record after 90 days. We may also remove strikes if the copyright infringement report is retracted or your appeal is approved." — [Help Center: Copyright](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/copyright)
- "We also reserve the right to immediately remove any account for a violation of our Community Guidelines, regardless of the number of strikes." — [Help Center: Copyright](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/copyright)
- Rejected appeal arguments: "The following appeal reasons, without any valid supporting evidence, are generally not accepted: Only a small portion of the work was copied and not the entire work. There are others who are also posting similar content. You don't claim to own the copyright to the content. You didn't know you weren't allowed to post the content without permission. Posting the content is protected under the right to freedom of speech." — [Help Center: Copyright](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/copyright)
- "Under certain circumstances, you may be legally permitted to use another person's copyrighted content without their permission. This includes fair use and exceptions to copyright such as quotation, criticism and/or review of the copyrighted content." — [Help Center: Copyright](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/copyright)
- "If you intend to use content that belongs to somebody else, it's advisable for you to obtain their permission beforehand... you may be allowed to use another party's copyrighted content without their permission. For example, if the content is in the public domain or is otherwise permitted by law. Keep in mind that a lack of knowledge isn't a valid defense against copyright infringement." — [Help Center: Copyright](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/copyright)
- Ban grounds list includes "Multiple violations of our Intellectual Property Policy." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- Creator Code of Conduct lists "violations of intellectual property rights" among prohibited behaviours that can restrict access to Creator Programs. (refreshed 2026-09-26) — [Creator Academy: Creator Code of Conduct](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-code-of-conduct)

**WRITTEN POLICY - Terms of Service (15 Jul 2026)**
- Users may not "use TikTok Content..., another user's content or generative AI-enabled features for commercial purposes unless permitted by TikTok USDS Joint Venture or the user, respectively". — [US Terms of Service](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/us/terms-of-service/en)

(search-result summary; page not opened) — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/clipping-creator-marketing-strategy/)
- Vendor commentary notes that clipping a segment of someone's video creates a derivative of copyrighted material and that campaigns "exist partly to give you that permission". (vendor source, search-result summary) — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/clipping/clipping-rights-and-permission.md)
- A legal-marketing blog on podcast DMCA takedowns states unauthorised clip accounts often crop, speed up or mirror clips to avoid detection and that this violates copyright and TikTok's terms. (search-result summary; not a court or regulator) — [PatentPC](https://patentpc.com/blog/dmca-takedown-requests-for-podcasts-protecting-audio-content)

### Inferences
- Podcast-clip channel: every clip without a licence is a removable item on the rightsholder's report; three removals inside a rolling 90-day window (not counting reports batched "within a short period") ends the account. A single podcaster running a sweep could batch into one strike - or several, at TikTok's discretion. The exposure is binary and outside the operator's control.
- The fair-use position of re-captioned, re-framed clips is weak on TikTok's own appeal criteria: it adds no criticism, commentary or review, and "only a small portion" is expressly listed as a non-accepted argument. A counter-notification also discloses the operator's contact details to the reporter.
- The realistic compliant routes for the clip channel are (a) written permission from each show, (b) joining an official clipping campaign/bounty where the rightsholder supplies assets, or (c) converting clips into genuine commentary/analysis with the operator's own narration occupying a substantial share of the runtime.
- Using clips also conflicts with ToS language on using "another user's content... for commercial purposes" where the source is a TikTok user's content, and blocks Creator Rewards (Section 8).
- Archive channel: films produced by US federal agencies (AEC/DOE, DoD) are outside US copyright, which is the strongest footing available. Residual risks are (1) Prelinger items that are not PD or CC0, (2) embedded commercial music or third-party stock within otherwise PD films, (3) contractor-produced films where copyright may have been retained or assigned, (4) third-party restorations/watermarked transfers, and (5) non-US jurisdictions where §105 does not apply by its terms. Per-film provenance logging (source URL, agency, item rights statement) is the evidence TikTok's appeal form asks for.
- Narration-plus-archive videos should use either no music, TikTok's library sounds, or properly licensed tracks under one minute if Creator Rewards is a goal.

### Gaps
- No TikTok statement or enforcement data specific to podcast-clip accounts was found.
- No US court decision on short-form podcast clipping was located in this session; the fair-use assessment above is inference from TikTok's criteria, not legal authority.
- Whether TikTok runs proactive audio/visual fingerprint matching for podcast video (as opposed to music) is undocumented.
- Whether TikTok's Commercial Music Library restrictions apply to Personal accounts that monetise was not checked.
- The IP Policy page reads "Released March 27, 2025 / Effective April 26, 2025" (confirmed in the raw page text); it is the oldest of the policy documents in force and predates the joint venture.

## 4. Automation and inauthentic behaviour: ToS and guidelines on scripts and bots, Content Posting API limits, spam and fake engagement, AI-written replies, detection and reported outcomes

### Takeaway
The August 2026 Community Guidelines state "We strictly prohibit automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems", define spam as "Using automation to run many accounts or send repetitive content" and prohibit "Using bots or scripts to write fake reviews or comments"; the only sanctioned automated posting route is the Content Posting API, which is private-only until audited and explicitly excludes "A utility tool to help upload contents to the account(s) you or your team manages". - UI automation of the official Android app over USB is a grey zone on the text: no clause names it, and the account is real, single-operator and posting through the genuine client. But the August 2026 sentence "We strictly prohibit automation tools, scripts, or other tricks designed to bypass our systems" is broad enough to be applied to it, and the operator's own use case is the one TikTok's API guidelines list as "Not acceptable" - so there is no sanctioned automated route for a private two-account pipeline other than TikTok Studio's own web scheduler/bulk upload (manual upload, automated publish time).
- The lowest-policy-risk automation design, on the written rules, is: render and caption automatically, then publish via TikTok Studio web scheduling, or keep the phone automation but confined to posting the operator's own videos at human-plausible cadence with no automated likes, follows, or unsolicited comments.
- Two posts per day per account is far below the API's own "typically around 15 posts per day" ceiling, so volume alone is not spam by TikTok's yardstick; "repetitive content" and "many accounts" are the triggers named.
- AI-drafted, human-approved comment replies on one's own videos are not prohibited by any text found: the prohibition targets "bots or scripts to write fake reviews or comments" and engagement inflation. Risk rises if replies are (a) posted without human approval, (b) near-identical across comments (repetitive content / comment spam down-ranking), (c) written to look like a different person or an organisation, or (d) posted via the automation at machine speed. The DM clause shows TikTok tolerates automated replies only as a business-account feature.
- If automation is detected or suspected, the documented consequences ladder is: verification challenges (CAPTCHA/SMS), restriction from posting, removal from top search results and FYF, ban, and bans of additional/associated accounts - plus Creator Rewards ineligibility under "Security issue" and "Platform Manipulation".

- The Developer Terms of Service and the audit application criteria were not read.
- TikTok's enforcement report does not, in the relayed coverage, break out actions for automation or spam posting specifically.

## 5. Multiple accounts on one device: limits and reach effects

### Takeaway
TikTok's rules expressly permit multiple accounts ("You can have multiple accounts... but not to deceive others or break the rules"); what is prohibited is using them to spread violations or evade restrictions, and enforcement can cascade to "associated accounts". The numeric per-device limit is not stated in any TikTok page I could read - third-party sources disagree (3, 5, up to 7-8) - and I found no evidence beyond vendor claims that two accounts on one phone reduces reach.

### Cited Findings
- "You can have multiple accounts—for example, for fan content or creative expression—but not to deceive others or break the rules." — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- "You can create multiple accounts on TikTok. However, we may restrict or permanently ban your account if you make any attempts to avoid a restriction or ban imposed on another account you own, including: Create a new account after your account has been banned for a severe violation. Post violative content on a new or existing account. Spread content violations across multiple accounts. This prohibition applies while any account restriction(s) or permanent account ban remains active on your other account." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- "If someone seriously breaks the rules or tries to dodge enforcement, we may ban all of their accounts, including associated accounts." — [Community Guidelines: Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features)
- Not allowed: "Using another account to avoid restrictions, such as comment blocks or FYF-ineligible content restrictions"; spam includes "Using automation to run many accounts". — [Community Guidelines: Integrity and Authenticity](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- Creator Code of Conduct prohibits "multiple-account abuse, or use of VPNs to circumvent our systems". — [Creator Academy: Creator Code of Conduct](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-code-of-conduct)
- IP Policy: "We reserve the right to refuse any account holder whose account was used for improper activities from opening a new TikTok account." — [TikTok IP Policy](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/copyright-policy/en)
- REPORTED / ANECDOTAL: third-party guides conflict on the in-app limit - one says the app allows "up to three accounts" per device, another "up to five", and one cites a user with seven working accounts on an iPhone; one claims "excessive switching may trigger security restrictions". (SEO/vendor blogs, search-result summaries) — [RecurPost](https://recurpost.com/blog/multiple-tiktok-accounts); [Framedrop](https://framedrop.ai/blog/how-many-tiktok-accounts-can-you-have); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-many-tiktok-accounts-can-you-run-at-once/)
- REPORTED / ANECDOTAL: vendors of anti-detect/multi-profile tools recommend separate devices or profiles per account "to reduce detection risks"; they have a commercial interest in that advice. — [Pixelscan](https://pixelscan.net/blog/tiktok-shadow-ban-guide-2025/)

### Inferences
- Two accounts on one phone is within what TikTok says is allowed. The material risk is linkage, not reach: because TikTok may ban "associated accounts" and treats cross-account spreading of violations as circumvention, a copyright or integrity ban on the podcast-clip account could take the nuclear-history account with it. Separating the higher-risk account onto its own device/login identity is risk isolation - but doing so in order to evade an active restriction would itself be a violation.
- Posting the same video, or continuing a restricted account's FYF-ineligible content, on the second account is the specific pattern named as circumvention.

### Gaps
- TikTok's Help Center article on adding/switching accounts could not be retrieved (the old URL resolves to a generic shell), so the official per-device account cap is unverified.
- No credible measurement of reach effects from co-located accounts or account switching exists in the sources found.
- How TikTok determines that accounts are "associated" (device ID, IP, payment, phone number) is not disclosed.

## 6. Sensitive-topic handling for nuclear-history and military niches

### Takeaway
Nuclear-test archive film and military interview talk are allowed under the August 2026 guidelines, with "Documentary or educational content" listed as allowed across the violence, weapons and dangerous-activity sections; the practical ceiling is that graphic imagery (bodies, burns, significant blood, combat killing) is either removed or age-restricted and FYF-ineligible even when in the public interest, and "showing or promoting... firearms or explosive weapons" sits in a restricted tier. A public-interest exception can keep content up while still making it ineligible for the For You feed.

**WRITTEN POLICY - Shocking and Graphic Content (effective 24 Sep 2026)**

When reviewing content, we consider if the post raises awareness or challenges harmful behavior. If you want to post content that does this, you might consider using features like captions, stickers, or voiceover to make it clear. Even if we allow content under a public interest exception, we might still: Make it ineligible for the FYF; Add a warning screen; Add a context label." — [Community Guidelines: Enforcement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/enforcement)
- Search: TikTok does not "recommend terms associated with harassment, sensationalism, or graphic and disturbing themes." — [Community Guidelines: Accounts and Features](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/accounts-features)
- Monetisation overlap: "Disturbing: Your account or content has graphics or scenes that could upset or shock viewers." is a Creator Rewards ineligibility factor. — [Creator Academy: Who is eligible](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/eligibility)
- Bans: permanent-ban triggers include "Promote or threaten violence" and "Post content that depicts real-world torture." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)

### Inferences
- Nuclear-test channel: mushroom clouds, test-site structures being destroyed, instrumentation and troop-observer footage contain no named prohibited element and fall within "Documentary"/"Educational". The red lines are Hiroshima/Nagasaki casualty footage (burned or dead bodies are NOT ALLOWED outright), medical footage of radiation injuries and human-subject tests (graphic injury), and animal-test footage (the Animal Abuse section restricts "graphic animal abuse or injury in a documentary, educational, or scientific context" and bans showing "charred, burned, or severely injured animals"). Those shots should be cut or blurred rather than relied on as "historical".
- The documentary exception is context-dependent and TikTok explicitly says narration ("voiceover"), captions and stickers are how context is established - which favours the narrated format - but the exception can still yield FYF ineligibility, i.e. zero recommendation reach with the video left up.
- "Explosive weapons" are named in the restricted "showing or promoting" tier. A nuclear device is plainly an explosive weapon in ordinary language; whether classifiers treat historic detonation footage as "showing... explosive weapons" is not stated. This is the main unquantified reach risk for channel 1.
- Military/special-operations podcast clips: combat descriptions are speech, not depiction, so the graphic-content rules bite mostly on any b-roll (firefight footage, kills, wounded). Risk areas are glorification ("praising acts of violence"), tactical "instructions", firearms shown on screen, statements about named groups that trip the violent-organisation rules without clear non-endorsement, and group-blaming or dehumanising language about enemy populations.
- Government-secrecy framing for nuclear history ("what they hid from you") edges toward the FYF-ineligible conspiracy tier ("assign blame to powerful groups or institutions, such as 'the government'") if claims are not sourced.
- Sensational titles and search terms ("graphic", "disturbing") are excluded from search recommendations, reducing search discoverability.

### Gaps
- No TikTok text addresses nuclear weapons or nuclear-test imagery specifically; treatment is inferred from general categories.
- Tier headings quoted in this section were confirmed against the page data, but TikTok does not say how "showing" a weapon is distinguished from documentary depiction of one; the Animal Abuse block restricting "graphic animal abuse or injury in a documentary, educational, or scientific context" is in the "FYF INELIGIBLE" tier.
- No enforcement data on how often documentary/educational exceptions are granted on appeal.

## 7. Account status tooling: seeing violations, appealing, and telling whether a video is FYF-ineligible

### Takeaway
TikTok provides three checks: Account check (TikTok Studio > More tools, or Safety Center) scans the account and the last 30 posts for restrictions and "posts not eligible for the feed"; per-video Analytics shows an ineligibility notice with an Appeal button (analytics must be switched on); and account status grades the last 90 days of violations as Healthy, Fair, Severe or Banned. Violations expire after 90 days and deleting a violating video does not erase the violation.

### Cited Findings
- "With just one click, Account check will scan your account and your last 30 posts for any issues involving: Login; Posts; Comments; Profile; Direct message. This includes restrictions from logging in, posting, commenting, editing your profile, or sending messages; posts not eligible for the feed; and posts receiving a violation." Path: "Tap Profile... Tap the Menu (☰) button... Select 'TikTok Studio.' Go to 'More tools' and tap 'Account check.'" "A green check mark will appear if no issues are discovered and a red exclamation point will appear if something does pop up!" (published 2026-06-16) — [Creator Academy: Account check](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/account-check)
- Alternative path: "From Safety Center: ...Settings and privacy. Tap Support. Tap Safety Center. Tap Account check." — [Help Center: Your account status](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/account-status)
- "Your account status reflects your violation history across TikTok over the last 90 days. Your account status can be Healthy, Fair, Severe, or Banned. You can view your account status and a record of your violations in account status." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- "Violations on your TikTok account will expire after 90 days and will no longer be taken into consideration." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- "The impact of a violation on your account status depends on the severity of the policy area involved... and the feature where the violation occurred... Repeated violations may progressively reduce your account status, lead to temporary restrictions on certain platform features..., or lead to a permanent account ban." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- Policy training: "For certain initial violations, you'll be given the opportunity to complete optional in-app policy training... If you are offered this training and you successfully complete it, eligible restrictions that were placed on your account may be lifted." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- FYF ineligibility notice: "we may determine your content is ineligible for recommendation in the For You feed. When this happens, your post won't be displayed in the For You feed. You can review the reason, submit feedback on our decision, and appeal if you believe we've made a mistake... Keep in mind that you'll need to turn on analytics in your TikTok settings to view our decision." Steps: "go to the post... Tap More insights at the bottom or tap the More options … button on the side of the post, then tap Analytics. Tap the notification for the ineligible post... Tap Appeal at the top." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- "If your content is made ineligible for the FYF or otherwise restricted, this information will appear in the TikTok analytics tool... Once your appeal is filed, you can view its status in the in-app Safety Center". — [Community Guidelines: Enforcement](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/enforcement)
- Violation notices: "In the TikTok app, tap Inbox. Tap System notifications. Tap Account updates." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- "Note: Deleting your violative content does not remove the violation, and we may still reduce your account status and/or impose restrictions after you delete violative content." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- Review triggers: content may be reviewed "when you upload content, if it gains popularity, or if it's reported." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- Low views are not proof of a restriction: "if one of your videos isn't getting many views, it doesn't necessarily mean it wasn't recommended for the For You feed." — [Creator Academy: Why is your video not getting recommended](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/video-not-recommended)
- Banned accounts: "you can log in to the account to submit an appeal and download your personal data." — [Help Center: Content violations and bans](https://support.tiktok.com/en/safety-hc/account-and-user-safety/content-violations-and-bans)
- Creator Rewards appeal windows: video ineligibility "within 80 days" and account disqualification "within 30 days" per Creator Academy; the Help Center says "All flags and disqualifications are eligible to be appealed within 30 days of receiving notice" and "The appeal review takes approximately 3 days"; Creator Academy says account appeal reviews take "up to 14 days" and "You may submit an appeal once per disqualification." — [Creator Academy: What videos are eligible](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/monetization-creativity-program-video-eligible); contradicted in part by [Help Center: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/creator-rewards-program)

### Inferences
- An automated pipeline can and should poll these surfaces: Account check covers only the last 30 posts, which at four posts a day across two accounts (two each) is a 15-day window per account - so it needs running at least fortnightly, and per-video Analytics notices are the only durable record.
- Because a second review happens "if it gains popularity", a video can be recommended initially and then be made ineligible once it takes off; a sudden mid-flight plateau is consistent with that mechanism, though TikTok says low views alone prove nothing.
- Deleting flagged videos does not help account status, so a "delete and repost" reflex in automation is counter-productive and could look like circumvention.

### Gaps
- TikTok does not publish how many violations move an account between Healthy, Fair and Severe, nor the thresholds for feature restrictions.
- No official statement on whether FYF-ineligibility decisions count as "violations" for account-status purposes (the Creator Rewards page counts "video violations" but does not define the term).
- Whether Account check and ineligibility notices are exposed through any API is unknown; they appear to be in-app only.

## 8. Monetisation: Creator Rewards Program requirements in 2026 and whether reused, AI-narrated or faceless content qualifies

### Takeaway
As of the September 2026 Creator Academy text the program requires a Personal account based and registered in the US, UK, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico or Brazil, age 18+, 10,000 followers and 100,000 views in the last 30 days, with videos over one minute that are "filmed, designed, and produced entirely by yourself". Faceless and TTS-narrated formats are not excluded by name, but content "reproduced from others with only slight modifications" or assembled from other sources "without new and personal ideas" is - which on the written text excludes the podcast-clip channel and makes the archive channel dependent on TikTok's reviewers accepting narration and editing as original input.

### Cited Findings

**WRITTEN POLICY - eligibility**
- "The program is currently open to creators in the United States, the United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico, and Brazil, so you must be based in one of these countries and have an account registered there." "Only Personal Accounts are eligible. Business Accounts, accounts associated with organizations, and accounts associated with political or government institutions do not qualify." "You need to be at least 18 years old to participate." "Your account should have at least 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days." "Keep it real by posting original content that's authentic to you." (2026-08-18) — [Creator Academy: Who is eligible](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/eligibility)
- Help Center adds: "Have authentic account information, such as your real name and date of birth." "Be at least 18 years old (or 19 in South Korea)." "Have a TikTok account in good standing, including no history of repeatedly or irresponsibly violating our Community Guidelines and Terms of Service policies and engaging in malicious or fraudulent activities." — [Help Center: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/creator-rewards-program)
- Third-party sites list additional countries (Canada, Italy, Spain, Australia) as eligible in 2026; this conflicts with TikTok's own September 2026 list of eight countries and should be treated as unreliable. — [messengerbot.app](https://messengerbot.app/id/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-2026-how-to-qualify-and-earn/); contradicted by [Creator Academy: Creator Rewards Program](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program)

**WRITTEN POLICY - video requirements and originality**
- "It must be original, high-quality content over 1 minute long. It should rack up at least 1,000 views on the For You feed. It can't be a Duet or Stitch. It can't be created using Photo Mode. Ads, paid promotions, or sponsored content do not qualify. All content must follow the program's terms, the Terms of Service, Community Guidelines, Creator Code of Conduct, and Copyright Policy." (2026-08-18) — [Creator Academy: What videos are eligible](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/monetization-creativity-program-video-eligible)
- "Post original and high-quality content that is filmed, designed, and produced entirely by yourself." "Be uploaded after joining the Creator Rewards Program." — [Help Center: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/creator-rewards-program)
- "Original content under the Creator Rewards Program is quality content that's designed, filmed, and produced by you that showcases your expertise, talent, or creativity... Content not considered original under the program includes, but is not limited to: Duet or Stitch videos. Content copied completely from others and with others' watermarks. Content reproduced from others with only slight modifications, including videos that are sped up or contain filters, fixed texts, or stickers. Content that contains different videos or pictures originating from other people, creators, or sources without new and personal ideas. Content that contains looping videos, single or multiple photos, or only text overlays. Content that contains lip syncs or copyrighted music that plays for over one minute." — [Help Center: Creator Rewards Program](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-rewards-program/creator-rewards-program)
- Account-review ineligibility factors: "Unoriginal content: Your account info or video content is copied from others or has minimal original input or edits. Low quality: Your account info or video content includes split screens, meaningless reactions, low-quality images, or slide videos. Advertising: Your profile or account info contains personal or business contact information. Clickbait:... - The four-factor formula "originality, play duration, search value and audience engagement" dates from the program's March 2024 launch; press at the time quoted TikTok describing originality as content "unique to the creator" showing "their point of view or creative thought process", play duration as combining watch time and finish rate, search value as reflecting how many users search for similar content, and engagement as likes, comments and shares. (secondary reporting of a TikTok announcement; TikTok's own "How are rewards calculated" article returned no content when fetched) — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/tiktoks-creator-rewards-program-57451/); [upGrowth](https://upgrowth.in/tiktoks-innovative-leap-the-latest-creator-rewards-program-and-its-emphasis-on-search-value/)
- Payout mechanics: redeemable "on the 15th of each month if they meet the $50.00 USD minimum payment threshold"; rewards estimate updates "within three days after you post a video". — [Creator Academy: Creator Rewards Program FAQs](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program-faq-2)
- Posting to the program does not change reach: "Your content's performance and your following won't be impacted when you join the program." — [Creator Academy: Creator Rewards Program FAQs](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/creator-rewards-program-faq-2)

**REPORTED / ANECDOTAL - demonetisation for "unoriginal content"**
- A May 2026 article reports creators being disqualified from Creator Rewards for violating "the originality policy" despite saying they filmed and edited everything themselves; it describes "thousands of accounts", "dozens of videos a day" of complaints, appeals "denied within hours with no human review and no detail on which specific video triggered the flag", and no public TikTok response. The outlet is an entertainment blog, the figures are unverified and no specific creators are named; no faceless or AI-voice accounts are mentioned. — [Baller Alert, 6 May 2026](https://balleralert.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-disqualification/)

### Inferences
- Podcast-clip channel: ineligible on the written text - not "filmed, designed, and produced entirely by yourself", and re-captioning/re-framing matches "only slight modifications, including... fixed texts, or stickers". Monetising it would have to come from clipping bounties or sponsorships, not Creator Rewards.
- Archive channel: not excluded by name, but the footage "originat[es] from other... sources", so eligibility hinges on demonstrating "new and personal ideas" - an original script, analysis, structure and design. Formats close to "looping videos, single or multiple photos, or only text overlays" or "slide videos" are excluded, so still-image-heavy edits are riskier than moving archive film with narration.
- The appeal evidence TikTok asks for (production photos, proof of filming/editing involvement) fits a camera-based creator; a faceless pipeline should retain script drafts, project files, source logs and render timelines to substitute.
- Geography is a hard gate: both the account's registration country and the viewers' countries must be among the eight; an operator based outside them cannot join regardless of content, and views from elsewhere earn nothing.
- The 60-second minimum conflicts with a short-clip format; twice-daily posting of sub-minute videos builds followers toward the 10,000/100,000 gate but those videos never earn.
- "Advertising: Your profile or account info contains personal or business contact information" means a bio with an email or link can itself be an ineligibility factor.
- Given the May 2026 reports of automated originality disqualifications hitting self-shot creators, a narrated-archive channel should expect false-positive risk at least as high.

### Gaps
- The legal Creator Rewards Program Terms could not be retrieved, so termination and clawback clauses are quoted from Help Center/Creator Academy only.
- TikTok publishes no statement on whether AI voice-over or faceless narration qualifies as original content; no first-party example was found.
- No verified, named case of a narrated public-domain-footage account being accepted into or removed from Creator Rewards was found.
- RPM levels and the weighting of the four factors are not published; the "originality / play duration / search value / audience engagement" wording could not be confirmed on a current TikTok page.

## 9. 2025-2026 changes from the US ownership change / joint venture

### Takeaway
The US Terms of Service (last updated 15 July 2026) are now an agreement with "TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC", which was formed when the deal closed on 22 January 2026 with ByteDance retaining 19.9%; the reported practical changes are a US-retrained recommendation algorithm and data hosted by Oracle. I found no evidence that the joint venture changed the substantive rules on AIGC, originality, automation or Creator Rewards - the Community Guidelines remain a single global document, updated in August 2026.

### Cited Findings
- "These Terms of Service ('Terms') are an agreement between you and TikTok USDS Joint Venture LLC ('TikTok USDS Joint Venture,' 'we,' or 'us'), the operator of the Platform in the United States". "Last updated: July 15, 2026". — [US Terms of Service](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/us/terms-of-service/en)
- The ToS attribute approvals and permissions to the joint venture (e.g. scraping "except as approved in writing by TikTok USDS Joint Venture") and refer to rewards programmes "offered by TikTok USDS Joint Venture". — [US Terms of Service](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/us/terms-of-service/en)
- Law-firm commentary: the deal closed 22 January 2026; "ByteDance holds 19.9% ownership"; Oracle manages US user data in its cloud; the algorithm is to be retrained on US data, with predicted "short-term instability in creator reach and metrics" and a possible long-term tilt toward domestic content; the new terms state the US entity "does not endorse any content" and require users to acknowledge the "risks and limitations of using generative AI". No monetisation changes are identified. (The reach predictions are the firm's opinion, not reported outcomes.) — [Romano Law](https://www.romanolaw.com/tiktoks-new-u-s-entity-explained-what-creators-need-to-know-in-2026/)
- News coverage: investors include Oracle, Silver Lake and MGX; Adam Presser (previously TikTok's head of operations and trust and safety) is CEO of the joint venture; users were prompted to accept new terms covering "new types of location information". (search-result summary of NBC-affiliate coverage; page not opened) — [NBC Washington](https://www.nbcwashington.com/news/national-international/what-to-know-about-tiktok-deal-us/4047453/)
- The Community Guidelines in force are a single global text ("People use TikTok in over 150 countries... we allow for regional applications of our guidelines"), released 25 August 2026 and effective 24 September 2026, hosted on the global tiktok.com domain and linking to the US Terms of Service. — [Community Guidelines: Overview](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/overview)
- The Creator Rewards qualified-view rule already confines payable views to eight countries, independent of the joint venture. — [Creator Academy: What counts as a qualified view](https://www.tiktok.com/creator-academy/en/article/monetization-creativity-program-qualified-view)

### Inferences
- For a US-registered account, the contracting and enforcing entity is now the joint venture, and trust-and-safety decisions for US users sit with it; the rulebook the operator must follow is unchanged in structure.
- If the operator is outside the US, their accounts remain under the non-US TikTok entity and terms (EEA/UK/ROW versions), which were not read here; clause wording on automation and scraping may differ slightly.
- The most plausible joint-venture effect on these two channels is distributional, not policy: US-trained ranking may change how well non-US-produced or US-history content travels between the US and other markets. This is speculative until measured.

### Gaps
- No primary TikTok/joint-venture statement was read on whether moderation models, appeal handling or originality classifiers differ between US and non-US users.
- Whether US and non-US accounts are now in separate recommendation pools (i.e. whether a non-US creator's video reaches US viewers as before) is not established by any source read; the law-firm piece is predictive.
- The January 2026 ToS diff versus the July 2026 version was not examined, so I cannot say what changed in the July update.
- The EEA/UK and "rest of world" Terms of Service were not checked for equivalent automation clauses.
