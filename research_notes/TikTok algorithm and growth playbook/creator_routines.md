# Creator routines: what high-view TikTok / short-form creators actually do day to day (2024-2026)

Researcher's note on evidence quality (read first): web search for this topic is dominated by SEO listicles and tool-vendor blogs. Named, first-person, dated evidence was thin, and I could not reach most paywalled press (Business Insider, WSJ, NYT, Digiday) or video/podcast transcripts. Each finding below is tagged:
- **[fetched]** = I opened the page and extracted the claim from it.
- **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result summary; not opened. Treat as a lead, not a verified fact.
- **[vendor]** = source sells a related tool/course; self-promotional.
- **[pre-2024]** = example predates the 2024-2026 window; included only because nothing newer and equally concrete was found.

## Daily and weekly routines: cadence, hours, time split

### Takeaway
There is no single "top creator" cadence: attributable examples range from one Short per week at 10-20 production hours each (Jenny Hoyos) to ~30 clips per day for automated clip accounts, with personality/lifestyle creators around 3 posts per day. I found no reliable dataset on how creators split hours between ideation, scripting, editing and engagement.

### Cited Findings
- Jenny Hoyos (YouTube Shorts, ~2M subs / ~850M views at the time): "I upload every week on the same day at the same time. I haven't missed a weekly upload this entire year." Production: "about 20 hours per Shorts. At a minimum, it's 10 hours." She plans monthly mixes ("a four-Shorts month" or "one long-form and three Shorts"). Dated 27 Sep 2023. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=176194)
- By March 2024 Hoyos was reported at 3M subscribers and ~10M average views per Short, i.e. low volume / high effort per piece. [fetched] — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Lydia Keating (1.3M across TikTok + Instagram): aims for 3 TikToks per day; a video takes from under 60 seconds to ~1 hour; ~3 brand collaborations per month with ~1 month turnaround; avoids her phone for 3-4 hours after waking. Dated 27 Jun 2022. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Techno Sapiens](https://technosapiens.substack.com/p/lydiakeating)
- Hunter Prosper (3.6M TikTok, full-time ICU night-shift nurse at the time): first hour of the day is phone-free; dedicates the first hour after posting to comments and DMs. Dated 27 Jan 2022. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/hunter-prosper-tiktok-1014/)
- Hogne ("Time Traveller POV", AI-generated history POV videos): ~8 hours per video. Dated 23 Feb 2025. [fetched] — [CO/AI summary of reporting](https://getcoai.com/?p=55436)
- Dylan Page ("News Daddy", 16M+ TikTok followers, started July 2020): some videos reportedly take a full day of research and editing. [snippet] — [Beacons bio](https://beacons.ai/i/blog/dylan-page-bio)
- Morning Brew Daily (podcast with a dedicated short-form video team): hosts start prep ~4:00 a.m., arrive 5:45, record at 6:00; moving recording from 9 a.m. to 6 a.m. "almost doubled" listenership "overnight" per co-host Toby Howell. Article dated 11 Aug 2026. [fetched] — [Talking Biz News](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/how-the-morning-brew-daily-podcast-is-created/)
- Clip account "KickClipper" (IShowSpeed clips): ~900 clips per month, i.e. ~30 per day (my division of the article's monthly figure), produced with an automated clipping tool. Dated 7 Jan 2025. [fetched] [vendor: the article is a tutorial for the vendor's own tool] — [Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/how-this-tiktok-clipper-made-300k-clipping-ishowspeed)
- Generic guidance that TikTok recommends 1-4 posts per day and that one batch session can yield 7-14 days of content is repeated across tool blogs; I did not find the primary TikTok statement. [snippet] [vendor] — [Logie](https://logie.ai/news/?p=8638), [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/guides/growth/tiktok-batch-content-creation/)

### Inferences
- The attributable examples cluster into three operating models: (a) craft model, about 1 post/week with 10-20 hours each; (b) personality model, 1-3 posts/day at minutes-to-an-hour each; (c) volume/clip model, tens of posts/day with near-zero marginal effort. The right benchmark depends on which model an account is running; a faceless documentary/history channel sits closer to (a) (8 hours per video for the AI-history example).
- Per-video effort and cadence trade off roughly inversely in these examples; none of the named creators combines high per-video effort with multiple posts per day without a team.

### Gaps
- No verified hour-by-hour time split (ideation vs. scripting vs. editing vs. engagement) for any named creator. Creator-economy surveys I reached did not publish hours per week.
- No 2024-2026 first-person routine from a large TikTok-native educational creator was retrieved; the best routine examples are 2022-2023.
- Dylan Page's posts-per-day and team size were not confirmed.

## Ideation systems: finding ideas, outliers, hooks, doubling down

### Takeaway
The best-documented system is competitor/outlier research plus comment mining before scripting, with the hook treated as the unit that decides the idea (Hoyos will change the whole video to get a stronger hook). Evidence that creators use TikTok Creative Center or search suggestions systematically came only from vendor blogs.

### Cited Findings
- Jenny Hoyos sits on ideas for long periods (some over a year), researches competitor videos, reads comments, and uses AI analysis to identify what works before producing; goal is "to make every Short hit Trending"; she evaluates hook, rewatchability, shareability and whether the content is actionable. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=176194)
- Hoyos hook rules: no more than 3 seconds, concise and visually pleasing, using "power words" such as "banned," "free," "one dollar," "secret," "cheap" (example: "$1 chicken sandwich"); immediately after the hook she foreshadows what is coming, what the conflict is and what to expect; uses "but/then" storytelling; ends on the strongest punchline/twist. Dated 19 Mar 2024. [fetched] — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Hoyos reportedly will change the idea of an entire video to get a strong hook, and judges hooks by viewed-vs-swiped-away percentage and overall retention. [snippet] — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Paddy Galloway (YouTube strategist; clients reported to include MrBeast and Sidemen) is described as building on outlier analysis and advising creators to adapt proven formats from unrelated niches (e.g. "$1 vs $100" comparisons). This is YouTube long-form focused, and the description comes from a tool vendor's profile of him. [snippet] [vendor] — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/paddy-galloway-growth-strategy/); see also [Colin and Samir resource page](https://www.colinandsamir.com/resources/the-new-rules-of-youtube-from-paddy-galloway)
- "James" of LivesandTimesHistory (115K+ TikTok followers about a year after starting): ideas began from remembered facts or snippets of history, which he then researched extensively before making the video. Date not confirmed. [snippet] — [Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/?p=3557)
- Colin Landforce (founder of Cut30, a short-form course) on the Nathan Barry Show, 18 Dec 2025: emphasises "one shot" videos, an "everyone's been asking me" hook, visual hooks and props, "track inputs, not outputs," and doing it again tomorrow. Results claims (students under 1,000 followers generating tens of thousands in revenue) are self-reported. [fetched] [vendor] — [Nathan Barry Show ep. 108](https://thenathanbarryshow.transistor.fm/episodes/colin-landforce)
- Morning Brew Daily's Toby Howell says the show's social "clips" are rerecorded, scripted and purpose-built for social rather than cut from the episode; the same discussion names topic selection as the critical variable. Dated 13 Apr 2026. [fetched] — [Signal Awards / The Signal Room](https://www.signalaward.com/2026/04/13/the-clip-economy-what-podcasters-are-getting-right-and-wrong/)

### Inferences
- Across Hoyos, Howell and Galloway the common pattern is that selection (idea/topic/hook) is treated as the highest-leverage step, ahead of editing polish. For a pipeline, this argues for spending disproportionate effort on topic + first-3-seconds selection and making the rest templated.
- "Outlier remake" is well attested as a YouTube practice; I found no named TikTok creator describing it first-hand in 2024-2026, only tools selling it.

### Gaps
- No named-creator evidence on use of TikTok Creative Center, search-suggestion mining, or formal idea banks.
- No documented hook A/B testing procedure on TikTok (e.g. reposting the same video with different hooks) from a named creator.
- No quantified evidence on "series" performance versus one-offs.

## Batch production and workflow: batching, templates, repurposing, editors, scheduling

### Takeaway
Documented production systems are either team-based daily shows (Morning Brew's dedicated short-form team) or fully templated/automated clip pipelines; solo-creator batching claims come almost entirely from tool vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Morning Brew's short-form team produces original daily sketches, explainers and on-location videos about the day's business headlines, separate from the podcast recording. [snippet] — [Shorty Awards entry](https://shortyawards.com/18th/morning-brew-daily)
- The KickClipper-style pipeline as described: paste a livestream URL into a clipping tool, pick a subtitle template, enable an auto "hook title," choose a square-in-vertical layout, auto-generate clips; volume (~900/month) substitutes for editing. [fetched] [vendor] — [Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/how-this-tiktok-clipper-made-300k-clipping-ishowspeed)
- Faceless production is commonly described as a repeatable chain: research, script, voiceover, edit, package, each step delegable or automatable. [snippet] [vendor] — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-growth-strategy/)
- Claim that batching 7-14 days of content in a 2-4 hour session cuts weekly production time by 60%: no methodology given. [snippet] [vendor] — [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/guides/growth/tiktok-batch-content-creation/)
- Hoyos plans output a month at a time rather than batching filming. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=176194)

### Inferences
- The only workflows with numbers attached are at the extremes (20 hours per Short; 30 automated clips a day). Mid-range "batch 10 videos on Sunday" advice is unverified.

### Gaps
- No verified data on cross-posting practice (TikTok vs. Reels vs. Shorts) or on which scheduling tools named creators use.
- No named examples of editor/team structure and cost for a solo-led short-form channel.

## Analytics habits: metrics, post-mortems, deciding what to make next

### Takeaway
The metrics creators and strategists name are retention/completion and the hook's view-vs-swipe rate; I found no first-person account of a structured TikTok post-mortem routine, and one creator describes compulsive checking as harmful.

### Cited Findings
- Hoyos targets 90%+ retention on Shorts. [fetched] — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Hoyos uses viewed-vs-swiped-away percentage and overall retention as her top measures of hook quality. [snippet] — [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Lydia Keating describes compulsively reloading to check video performance as "really unhealthy" and tries to stay off her phone for hours after posting. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Techno Sapiens](https://technosapiens.substack.com/p/lydiakeating)
- TikTok's analytics expose: a per-video retention curve, "Watched full video" %, average watch time, traffic source types (For You, profile, search, other) and a follower-activity view by hour and day. [snippet] — [HEC Montreal digital blog](https://digital.hec.ca/en/blog/tiktok-analytics-to-optimize-your-reach/)
- Third-party completion benchmarks (80%+ under 15s; 60%+ for 15-30s; 50%+ for 30s+) circulate, with no disclosed dataset. [snippet] [vendor] — [Mega Digital](https://megadigital.ai/en/blog/tiktok-retention-rate/)
- Creator Rewards RPM is described as depending on originality, play duration, engagement and search value, which makes search traffic a monetisation metric as well as a reach metric. [snippet] — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/tiktoks-creator-rewards-program-57451/)
- Landforce's advice is to "track inputs, not outputs" (count what you publish rather than obsess over results). [fetched] [vendor] — [Nathan Barry Show](https://thenathanbarryshow.transistor.fm/episodes/colin-landforce)

### Inferences
- The two best-attested decision metrics map directly onto TikTok's dashboard: early drop-off on the retention curve (hook) and watched-full-video % (payoff). A post-mortem built on those two plus traffic source is consistent with what is documented, but is my construction, not a described creator routine.

### Gaps
- When creators check analytics (e.g. 1h / 24h / 7d) and how they run post-mortems: no named evidence.
- No verified benchmark for "good" retention on 60s+ TikToks.

## Engagement habits: comments, pins, video replies, lives, community

### Takeaway
The one attributable habit is a dedicated first hour after posting for comments and DMs (Hunter Prosper, 2022); whether fast replies cause reach is not established by anything I found.

### Cited Findings
- Hunter Prosper (3.6M) dedicates the first hour after posting to replying to comments and DMs, and says it is time-consuming alongside a full-time nursing job. 27 Jan 2022. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/hunter-prosper-tiktok-1014/)
- Hoyos reads comments (her own and competitors') as research input for future videos. [fetched] [pre-2024] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=176194)
- A stock-media company blog poses the question of whether faster replies boost reach; I did not open it and cannot report its conclusion. [snippet] [vendor] — [Uppbeat](https://fastly-f.uppbeat.io/blog/creator-questions/do-faster-comment-replies-actually-boost-your-reach)

### Inferences
- Comments function in the documented cases more as an idea source and audience-relationship habit than as a proven ranking lever.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 named evidence on time spent on engagement, pinned-comment tactics, video replies to comments, going live, or engaging with other accounts in a niche.
- No causal evidence that first-hour replies increase distribution.

## Faceless / archive-footage / documentary-history short-form channels

### Takeaway
Named history successes on TikTok are mostly on-camera historians; the documented faceless history successes are AI-generated POV channels (one video at 53M views, ~8 hours of work each) that historians criticise for inaccuracy. I found no first-person process account from a faceless archive-footage history channel.

### Cited Findings
- Dan ("POV Lab") and Hogne ("Time Traveller POV") make AI-generated first-person "waking up in [period]" videos; one Black Death video reached 53M views; multiple AI tools generate images, animation and sound; ChatGPT is used for research; detailed prompts are needed to prevent anachronisms; Hogne spends ~8 hours per video; Dan says he prioritises evoking atmosphere over complete accuracy. 23 Feb 2025. [fetched; this is a summary of press reporting, not the original] — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/?p=55436)
- Historians' criticism of those videos: Dr. Amy Boyington called them "amateurish" and warned of potential for historical manipulation (glazed windows and train tracks in 1300s scenes); Dr. Hannah Platts noted modern bread loaves and wrong dining customs in Pompeii videos; Prof. Elizabeth Frood said they oversimplify. [fetched] — [CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/?p=55436)
- On-camera history creators: Rosie Harte (nearly 500K TikTok followers, started in lockdown, book deal, works with museums on social content) — [Bristol24/7](https://www.bristol247.com/lifestyle/fashion/meet-the-bristol-tiktoker-bringing-royal-fashion-to-life); Shermann "Dilla" Thomas (Chicago history in 60 seconds) — [Chicago Defender](https://chicagodefender.com/tiktok-historian-shermann-thomas-will-school-you-in-60-seconds/); James of LivesandTimesHistory (115K+ in about a year) — [Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/?p=3557). All [snippet]; dates and current follower counts not verified.
- Claims that faceless channels were "more than 45% of top YouTube channels by subscriber growth" in 2025 and that faceless TikTok accounts generated "over 18 billion views per month by late 2025" come from an AI-video tool's blog with no visible methodology. Treat as unverified. [snippet] [vendor] — [EasyViral](https://easyviral.ai/api/blog-md/faceless-content-taking-over-social-media)
- Vendor descriptions of faceless history channels cite "dramatic AI voiceover and atmospheric AI-generated imagery" on hidden events / dark chapters / little-known stories. [snippet] [vendor] — [Flowshorts](https://flowshorts.app/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel/history)

### Inferences
- The faceless-history format with documented scale is "immersive POV," not narrated archive footage; its hook is experiential ("you wake up in...") rather than informational.
- Accuracy is the visible reputational risk for AI-assisted history content; factual checking is a differentiator that the criticised channels lack.
- Faceless, AI-voiced, reused-footage content also sits closest to TikTok's unoriginal-content rules (see clipping section), so originality of script and visuals is a monetisation issue, not only a quality one.

### Gaps
- No verified examples (with process and growth timeline) of faceless archive-footage documentary channels on TikTok; no evidence on human vs. AI voice performance, caption style, words-per-minute or shot pacing from named channels.
- Zack D. Films and similar Shorts-native faceless channels: search returned nothing usable.

## Podcast clipping operations

### Takeaway
Clipping has industrialised into pay-per-view bounty networks (reported rates $0.50-$25 per 1,000 views; networks of 700 to 40,000 clippers), and clips routinely out-reach the source show by an order of magnitude; but clip accounts generally do not qualify for TikTok's own monetisation and are exposed to unoriginal-content enforcement.

### Cited Findings
- Scale and economics, from a newsletter synthesis dated 18 May 2026: Emrah Bayraktar (25, Antwerp) runs a network of 40,000 freelance clippers; Bo Lucenko (19) earns ~$4,000/month clipping for tech founders; Cluely CEO Roy Lee hired 700 clippers paid per view and reported 1B+ views in a month; bounty marketplaces include Content Rewards and Vyro; Polymarket ran a $70,000 bounty at $0.50 per 1,000 views; MLB clips paid $1 per 1,000; AI-startup product clips up to $25 per 1,000; industry estimated at ~$3B in early 2026. [fetched; secondary synthesis, underlying reporting not checked] — [Shira's newsletter](https://shiras-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/we-re-all-living-in-the-clipping-economy)
- Clip-vs-source reach, same source: TBPN has ~7,000 live viewers while clips average ~257,000 views (37x); Hasan Piker averages ~33,000 live viewers vs. 700,000+ average clip views. [fetched] — [Shira's newsletter](https://shiras-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/we-re-all-living-in-the-clipping-economy)
- Critique in the same piece: ad executive Lou Paskalis called it "a perfectly terrible problem" (disposable content for consumers, non-converting impressions for advertisers, monetisation lost to middlemen). [fetched] — [Shira's newsletter](https://shiras-newsletter.beehiiv.com/p/we-re-all-living-in-the-clipping-economy)
- Law-firm explainer, 22 Oct 2025: clipping = taking podcasts, interviews or full-length videos and cutting short highlight segments, paid per 1,000 views; reports of some clippers earning $20,000-$30,000 per month; notes the shift away from the traditional licence-and-approve IP model toward rights-holders enabling distributed editing. [fetched] — [Loeb & Loeb Quick Takes](https://www.loeb.com/quicktakes/post/102lqhl/)
- Selection principle: official show accounts treat topic selection as decisive; Morning Brew Daily's "clips" are rerecorded and scripted for social; 71% of Gen Z reportedly discover new shows through short-form video; open question raised by Bloomberg's Ashley Carman and Vox Media's Nishat Kurwa is whether clips win new listeners or cannibalise the core audience. 13 Apr 2026. [fetched] — [Signal Awards](https://www.signalaward.com/2026/04/13/the-clip-economy-what-podcasters-are-getting-right-and-wrong/)
- A clip "has to contain a complete idea that makes sense to someone who has never heard of the show." [snippet] — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-to-clip-a-podcast)
- My First Million reportedly launched a "clipper army" after an earlier push yielded 20M impressions in a month. [snippet; third-party summary] — [Summify](https://summify.io/discover/we-hit-record-on-a-private-strategy-session-D8pkn3/)
- KickClipper: ~900 clips/month of IShowSpeed streams; "$300,000 in one month" is explicitly an estimate by the tool vendor; stated success factors are choosing creators who encourage clipping and posting regularly. 7 Jan 2025. [fetched] [vendor] — [Ssemble](https://www.ssemble.com/blog/how-this-tiktok-clipper-made-300k-clipping-ishowspeed)
- Typical campaign rates are reported at up to $5 per 1,000 verified views, with cheap briefs near $0.20; "consistent clippers hitting $1,000 to $2,000 a month within about 90 days" is a vendor claim. [snippet] [vendor] — [Wayin](https://wayin.ai/blog/make-money-clipping-videos-on-tiktok/)
- Originality rules: Creator Rewards requires 10K followers, 100K views in 30 days and original videos over one minute; duets, stitches, reposts, slideshows and watermarked reused content are excluded; "most clip accounts will never qualify," so clipper income comes from external campaigns that count TikTok views. [snippet] [vendor] — [OpenClip](https://openclip.app/guides/clipping-on-tiktok)
- TikTok reportedly tightened enforcement on unoriginal content from 15 Sep 2025 (violation points, commission freezes, reduced visibility); the source is a seller-tools blog, so this may be specific to TikTok Shop creators. [snippet] [vendor] — [BigSeller](https://www.bigseller.pro/blog/articleDetails/3778/tiktok-unoriginal-content.htm)

### Inferences
- The economics of clipping are paid by the source (show, brand, startup) for reach, not by TikTok. A clip account without a paying campaign or its own product has no native TikTok revenue path.
- Two distinct clip strategies exist: distributed volume (hundreds or thousands of clippers, low selection quality, pay per view) and official "made-for-social" segments (rerecorded, scripted). The second is the one compatible with originality rules.
- The 37x live-to-clip multiple suggests the clip is the primary product for discovery; conversion from clip view to long-form listener is contested and unquantified.

### Gaps
- No first-hand description from a clipping team of moment-selection criteria, title/caption conventions or subtitle style with performance data.
- No primary TikTok policy text was retrieved; originality rules above are via third-party summaries.
- FTC disclosure obligations for paid clippers were not covered by the sources I reached.

## Realistic growth timelines and base rates

### Takeaway
Named examples take from about a year to several years, and base rates are harsh: in a 3,000-creator survey, half earned under $15K a year and about 57% of full-timers earned below a $44K living wage. Published timelines are survivorship-biased and I found no denominator for how many accounts ever reach large audiences.

### Cited Findings
- Influencer Marketing Hub Creator Earnings Report 2025 (3,000+ creators surveyed, 1.1B+ combined followers): 50% earn under $15K/year (up from 48% in 2023); nearly 57% of full-time creators earn under $44,000 from content; 43.8% have under 100K followers; brand deals are the main revenue source for 49%; only 1 in 4 have formal management; most respondents have 1-4 years' experience; 44% are primarily on TikTok. The page was dated 6 Jul 2026 when fetched (probably an update date). [fetched] — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/)
- Same report as summarised elsewhere: 5.7% earn $100K+; 27% consider creation their full-time job. This conflicts with the fetched page's statement that more than half treat it as full-time; the two figures may use different definitions, and I could not reconcile them. [snippet] — [Blotato](https://www.blotato.com/blog/how-much-do-content-creators-make) vs. [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/creator-earnings-report-2025/)
- Jenny Hoyos: began uploading in 2021 expecting nobody to watch; nearly 2M subscribers and ~850M views by Sep 2023; 3M by Mar 2024. [fetched] — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/?p=176194), [Marketing Examined](https://marketing-examined.beehiiv.com/p/jenny-hoyos-shortform-video-playbook)
- Dylan Page: started July 2020; 16M+ followers by the time of his bio. [snippet] — [Beacons](https://beacons.ai/i/blog/dylan-page-bio)
- James (LivesandTimesHistory): 115K+ followers roughly one year after starting. [snippet] — [Net Influencer](https://netinfluencer.com/?p=3557)
- One creator's account of posting daily for 284 days (Jan-Nov 2023): first viral video hit 20K views overnight and eventually 230K+ views, i.e. daily posting for most of a year produced a modest outlier, not a breakout. [snippet] — [search result, source among Substack posts such as This Is Bokeh](https://thisisbokeh.substack.com/p/in-my-tiktok-era)

### Inferences
- Every timeline above comes from someone who succeeded and was therefore interviewed. The survey's respondents are themselves a selected group (self-identified creators, many with management or large followings), so even the 50%-under-$15K figure overstates typical outcomes for someone starting today.
- A planning assumption consistent with the evidence is one to three years of consistent output before large scale, with no guarantee.

### Gaps
- No data on what share of TikTok accounts reach 10K / 100K / 1M followers.
- No named creator gave an exact count of posts before breakout.
- Linktree, Kit and Goldman Sachs reports were not retrieved.

## What creators say changed in 2024-2026

### Takeaway
Two shifts are documented: monetisation moved to original 60s+ videos under the Creator Rewards Program (RPM around $0.40-$1.20 in the reports I found), and the January 2026 US ownership change was followed by creator reports of glitches, zero-view videos, suppression and falling payouts, which TikTok/Oracle attributed to a data-centre outage.

### Cited Findings
- Creator Rewards pays on qualified views of videos over one minute, for accounts with 10K+ followers and 100K+ views in the last 30 days; RPM is said to weigh originality, play duration, engagement and search value. [snippet] — [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/tiktoks-creator-rewards-program-57451/), [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/guides/creator-rewards-program/)
- A creator with 55,000 followers reported an RPM of $1.20 per 1,000 qualified views; Simon Owens' comment: "nowhere close to great, but it's also a lot better than the rate TikTok paid out during its Creator Fund era." 1 Nov 2024. [fetched] — [Simon Owens](https://simonowens.substack.com/p/has-tiktok-improved-its-revenue-share)
- Typical RPM ranges quoted as $0.40-$1.00+ versus $0.02-$0.04 under the old Creator Fund. [snippet] [vendor] — [ttcalculator](https://ttcalculator.net/blog/how-tiktok-creator-rewards-work-2026/)
- US ownership timeline: sale announced 22 Dec 2025; deal closed 22 Jan 2026 with new terms of service; from 25 Jan 2026 users reported glitches, suppression and demonetisation. Dylan Page reported political video suppression and demonetisation; Amanda "Swell Entertainment" Golka reported multiple videos stuck at zero views for hours; 64% of Publish Press readers polled noticed changes to their For You Page; app uninstalls reportedly rose 130%; Oracle attributed the issues to a data-centre outage. 28 Jan 2026. [fetched] — [The Publish Press](https://news.thepublishpress.com/p/something-s-off-with-tiktok)

### Inferences
- Because Creator Rewards pays only on original 60s+ videos and counts search value, the incentive since 2024 points toward longer, search-titled, original-script videos, which is the opposite of the high-volume clip model.
